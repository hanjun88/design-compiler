00003	05 Composite Chart vs Synastry Chart: Which Reveals More About Your Relationship?
00004	Source: SynastryChart (synastrychart.org) guide
00005	URL: https://synastrychart.org/blog/composite-chart-vs-synastry-chart
00006	
00007	Two people sit across from an astrologer and ask: What do our charts say about us? The astrologer pulls up two different charts, and the couple is confused.
00008	One chart overlays two wheels on top of each other; the other blends them into a single wheel. They look nothing alike. They seem to tell different stories.
00009	That is because they do.
00010	A synastry chart takes two natal charts on top of each other without merging them and shows how two individuals interact.
00011	A composite chart shows what the relationship itself looks like as its own entity.
00012	Both are valuable. Neither is complete on its own.
00013	Choosing the wrong one for the wrong question leads to readings that feel off.
00014	What Is a Synastry Chart?
00015	A synastry chart places two natal charts on top of each other without merging them.
00016	You see Person A's planets in one color and Person B's planets in another, and you look at the aspects (angles) between them.
00017	It is a dialogue chart.
00018	It shows how your Mars talks to their Venus, how your Moon sits relative to their Saturn, where the friction is, where the flow is.
00019	The two individuals stay separate; you are reading the conversation between them.
00020	What Synastry Is Best For: understanding how two people experience each other, and identifying areas of attraction, tension, and growth.
00021	It is also useful for seeing which parts of your chart get activated by a specific person, especially in early-stage relationships.
00022	Synastry answers the question: What happens when we are in the same room?
00023	What Is a Composite Chart?
00024	A composite chart takes the mathematical midpoint between every pair of corresponding planets, the midpoint of both Suns, both Moons, both Mercurys, and so on, and plots them as a single chart.
00025	The result is one wheel that represents the relationship as its own entity.
00026	The idea, first popularized by John Townley in the 1970s and refined by Robert Hand in Planets in Composite, is that a relationship is more than just two people reacting to each other.
00027	It has its own personality, its own needs, its own trajectory; the composite chart maps that.
00028	What the Composite Is Best For: understanding the nature of the relationship itself; seeing the relationship's strengths, challenges, and purpose.
00029	It is especially useful for long-term relationships where the we has become its own thing.
00030	The composite answers: What does this relationship want to become?
00031	The Core Difference, Side by Side
00032	Synastry maps two individuals interacting; the composite maps the relationship as one entity.
00033	Synastry uses two overlaid wheels with direct aspect comparison; the composite uses one merged wheel with the midpoint of each planet pair.
00034	The best metaphor for synastry is a conversation between two people; for the composite it is a portrait of the couple.
00035	The short version: synastry shows how you relate; the composite shows what you are together.
00036	They are not competing tools; they are complementary lenses.
00037	How Each Chart Is Calculated
00038	Synastry: Direct Overlay. No calculation happens between the two charts; you place Chart A and Chart B on the same wheel and measure the angles.
00039	If your Sun is at 15 Aries and their Moon is at 17 Leo, that is a trine (120 degrees apart, within 2 degree orb). The skill is in reading aspect patterns, not in computing anything.
00040	Composite: Midpoint Blending. Every planet pair gets averaged.
00041	If your Sun is at 10 Aries and your partner's Sun is at 20 Gemini, the composite Sun sits at 15 Taurus, the zodiacal midpoint.
00042	The same calculation runs for every planet, the Ascendant, and the Midheaven.
00043	This creates a chart that neither person owns. It belongs to the relationship.
00044	What Each Chart Is Best For, By Situation
00045	Dating or Early Relationship (0 to 6 months): Use synastry; the composite will feel abstract because the we has not fully formed yet.
00046	Committed Relationship (6 months to 3 years): Use both; read the composite for the big-picture direction and the synastry for the day-to-day texture.
00047	Long-Term or Marriage (3+ years): Lead with the composite; the relationship genuinely is its own entity, and synastry is used to troubleshoot specific friction points.
00048	Non-Romantic Relationships: Both work; synastry is excellent for business partnerships, friendships, and parent-child dynamics.
00049	John Townley originally intended the composite for all relationship types, not just romantic ones.
00050	A Rule of Thumb
00051	If you are asking why does this person affect me this way, read the synastry.
00052	If you are asking what is this relationship becoming, read the composite.
00053	Reading a Synastry Chart, 5 Focus Areas
00054	Sun-Moon Cross-Aspects: the backbone of emotional compatibility; one person's Sun conjunct or trine the other's Moon is one of the strongest indicators of mutual understanding and long-term warmth.
00055	Venus-Mars Aspects: the chemistry axis; conjunctions and trines flow easily, squares create intense, sometimes volatile passion.
00056	Saturn Aspects: the glue, or the weight; they bring stability and commitment but also restriction and pressure, and are common in long-term relationships.
00057	House Overlays: where one person's planets fall in the other's chart colors which life domains the relationship activates.
00058	Planets in your 7th house directly impact the partnership; planets in your 12th stir unconscious material.
00059	Outer Planet Contacts: Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto aspects to personal planets create generational or transformative dynamics.
00060	These are slow-burn deep structural themes rather than daily interaction.
00061	Reading a Composite Chart, 5 Focus Areas
00062	The composite is read like a natal chart, but for the relationship.
00063	Composite Sun: The Relationship's Identity. The sign and house of the composite Sun describe what the relationship is about; composite Sun in the 10th means a public, achievement-oriented identity, in the 4th means anchored in home and family.
00064	Composite Moon: The Emotional Baseline. It describes how the relationship feels on a daily basis, warm and nurturing, independent and cerebral, or intense and transformative.
00065	Composite Venus: How Love Is Expressed. It shows the relationship's love language, what the couple enjoys together, how they express affection, and what they value as a unit.
00066	Composite Saturn: The Relationship's Challenges. It points to the area where the relationship faces its most persistent difficulties, and where the most growth happens if both people are willing.
00067	Composite Saturn in the 7th house, ironically, is common in marriages that last.
00068	Composite Angles (ASC and MC). The composite Ascendant is how the relationship presents itself to the outside world; the composite Midheaven is the relationship's public direction or shared ambition.
00069	The Composite Planet table: Sun is identity and purpose; Moon is emotional baseline; Mercury is communication style; Venus is love expression and shared values; Mars is shared drive and conflict style; Jupiter is growth areas; Saturn is challenges, commitment, structure; Ascendant is how others see the relationship.
00070	Real Example, Beyonce and Jay-Z
00071	Their synastry shows a strong Venus-Mars interaction, physical chemistry and romantic attraction directly wired.
00072	Their Moons and Suns are in aspect, giving deep emotional recognition, alongside Saturn aspects that bring seriousness and heaviness.
00073	Their composite chart paints a bigger picture: the composite Sun activates themes of power, legacy, and public identity.
00074	The composite Moon suggests an emotionally intense baseline, and composite Saturn is prominent.
00075	The synastry explains why they were drawn to each other.
00076	The composite explains what the relationship became, a power partnership with a public identity and a shared purpose that extends beyond romance.
00077	Neither chart alone tells the full story; together, they do.
00078	The Davison Chart, the Third Option
00079	The Davison Relationship Chart, named after Ronald Davison, takes a different approach from the composite.
00080	Instead of calculating mathematical midpoints, it finds the midpoint in time and space between two births, then casts a chart for that real moment and place.
00081	The result is a real astronomical chart, an actual sky that existed, rather than the abstract midpoints of a composite.
00082	Common Mistakes
00083	Treating synastry and composite as the same thing: they measure different dimensions of the relationship; reading one does not replace reading the other.
00084	Only reading the composite for new relationships: the composite describes potential that has not been activated yet; early on, synastry is usually more felt.
00085	Ignoring composite house placements: the composite Sun's house placement is arguably the single most important thing in the chart.
00086	Panicking over a bad composite chart: a composite with lots of squares and Saturn contacts does not mean a doomed relationship.
00087	It means the relationship has structure, challenges, and growth potential; many lasting marriages have difficult composite charts.
00088	Forgetting that both charts are static: neither synastry nor the composite shows how the relationship evolves over time.
00089	Frequently Asked Questions
00090	Which is more accurate, synastry or composite? Neither is; they measure different things, like a thermometer and a barometer.
00091	Can I use a composite chart for a friendship? Yes; composite charts work for any relationship that has its own identity, regardless of type.
00092	Do I need birth times for both charts? For synastry you can keep planetary aspects without exact times; for a composite, birth times are more important because the Ascendant and houses are central.
00093	What if our synastry looks great but our composite looks terrible?
00094	Strong synastry with a challenging composite often describes a couple with fantastic chemistry who struggle with the relationship as a shared project.
00095	That is information, not a death sentence.
00096	Use Both, They are Complementary
00097	Synastry and composite charts are not rivals; they are two views of the same relationship, taken from different altitudes.
00098	Synastry is street-level, the eye contact, the arguments, the chemistry.
00099	The composite is aerial, the shape of the thing, its direction, its meaning.
00100	For a full reading, you need both: start with the synastry to understand the people, then move to the composite to understand the partnership.
