00018	KEY CONCEPT #1:  ENTRAPMENT
00019	One of the defining characteristics of abuse is that you can’t easily get away from it. If you could, you would.
00020	The entrapment element is always present. Intimate partners are trapped by threats, by isolation, by lack of access to money, by not wanting to get separated from their children, by brainwashing, and by a long list of other factors. For example, when a woman has children with a man who is abusing her, leaving him means that she faces giving up a lot of her time with her kids, because the abuser will seek visitation or even primary custody.
00021	My central message to the abused woman:
00022	Reflect on the factors that are trapping you. Don’t blame yourself for being trapped. You can find a way out, but it will take some time. The better you understand what’s holding you there, the better the chance of figuring out your path to freedom.
00023	My central message to the support person
00024	:
00025	Women don’t get stuck in abuse because of masochism. They get stuck because the abuser — along with the society that backs that abuser up — has trapped them in a bunch of different ways, not least of which is the huge trauma he has caused.  Offering her simple solutions won’t help, because there are no simple solutions. You become much more helpful when you grasp how complex it all is.
00026	KEY CONCEPT # 2:  ABUSERS TELL YOU IT’S YOUR OWN FAULT
00027	An abuser won’t necessarily blame you for every single incident  – he may even act very apologetic sometimes — but nonetheless he is determined overall to drum it into you that you’re the real cause of how he acts.
00028	The result is that virtually every abused woman comes to feel that she is at fault. She feels that she pushed the abuser’s buttons, or that she caused him to turn mean, or that it’s her fault because she didn’t leave. All of these messages are the voice of the abuser speaking through her mind.
00029	In reality, nothing an abused woman can do will make the abuser treat her any better. Abusive behavior comes
00030	entirely
00031	from factors that are
00032	internal
00033	to the abuser, and not at all from anything about the personality, behavior, or responses of the targeted person.
00034	My central message to the abused person
00035	:
00036	You are doing nothing – zero – to cause the way you’re being treated. The abuser is 100% responsible for his own behavior. None of what he does is your fault, and you can’t make his behavior change by changing yours.
00037	My central message to the support person
00038	:
00039	Blaming an abused woman for the abuser’s behavior, or for the fact that she hasn’t left,
00040	never
00041	helps. The way out comes from supporting and empowering the target of the abuse, not by blaming or criticizing her.
00042	KEY CONCEPT #3:  YES, IT IS THAT BAD
00043	I very often hear targets of abuse saying, “I’m probably making too big a deal about this,” or, “I’m sure I’m blowing this all out of proportion, it’s not really so bad.” This is, again, the voice of the abuser speaking through her; the abuser loves to brainwash the woman to believe that she’s hysterical, that she overreacts, and that she’s unstable. Don’t buy it.
00044	My central message to the abused person:
00045	Take your feelings seriously. When someone tells you that you’re overreacting to how they’re treating you, that in itself is a bad sign about them. Trust your own instincts.
00046	My central message to the support person
00047	:
00048	Keep validating the person’s feelings. Keep pointing out her strengths and accomplishments. Support targets of abuse to believe in themselves.
00049	KEY CONCEPT #4:  ISOLATION IS AN ENEMY, CONNECTION IS A FRIEND
00050	Abuse thrives where people feel alone. And abusers work hard to keep their targets isolated.
00051	Don’t keep telling yourself, “I can handle this, I can handle this.” That message may help you be strong sometimes, but right now it’s not working; in fact, it’s keeping you vulnerable to the abuser.
00052	It’s okay to need help
00053	. In fact, it’s profoundly human to give and receive assistance.
00054	Find someone to whom you can tell the truth about what’s happening. Take the leap to trust someone. Build your social connection in whatever way you can. It may take creativity and courage to break out of isolation, but it’s the most important step you can take to change your future.
00055	My central message to the abused person
00056	:
00057	You don’t have to be alone and it isn’t good for you. Don’t listen to the abuser when he blames you for why you feel so alone. Keep finding ways to reach out to people and never give up.
00058	My central message to the support person:
00059	Connect, connect, connect. Be there, be there, be there. Helping the abused woman overcome isolation is the most valuable contribution you can make. Work to govern your own frustration and impatience because those will keep you from being fully connected to her.
00060	KEY CONCEPT #5:  YOU WILL FIND YOUR WAY OUT
00061	The abuser works hard to convince you that you’re incompetent, selfish, and messed up, and that no one cares about you. And at times what he’s saying may even start to seem true, as if he’s turning you into the bad things he says you are.
00062	Try to see through these lies and toxic dynamics; there is nothing wrong with you.
00063	You will find a way to an abuse-free life, and to heal from the harm that he’s done.
00064	The great majority of women living with abuse get out eventually. It can feel like you’ll be there forever, but you won’t.
00065	My central message to the abused person:
00066	You are smart, capable, and sane. You will get free, and be surrounded once again by people who love you, believe in you, and are kind to you.
00067	My central message to the support person
00068	:
00069	Keep reflecting back to the abused person all of the positive qualities you see in her. Keep believing that she will get free.
