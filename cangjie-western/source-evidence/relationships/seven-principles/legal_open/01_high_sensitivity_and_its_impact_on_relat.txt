00004	Understand what it means to be a highly sensitive person and how to build emotionally secure relationships using Gottman insights.
00005	Est. reading time: 4 min.
00006	What Is High Sensitivity?
00007	In psychology, high sensitivity refers to a personality trait known as Sensory Processing Sensitivity (SPS) — the defining feature of being a Highly Sensitive Person (HSP). This concept was first researched and popularized by psychologist Dr. Elaine Aron
00008	in the 1990s. High sensitivity is characterized by:
00009	Feeling emotions (both positive and negative) more intensely
00010	Becoming overstimulated easily
00011	Increased awareness of other people’s emotions
00012	Needing more downtime
00013	Deeply thinking about and processing experiences and emotions
00014	How Common Is High Sensitivity?
00015	High sensitivity is not a disorder but rather a personality trait. It is equally present in men and women and is found in 20-30% of the population. Research (including twin and family studies by Dr. Elaine Aron, Dr. Michael Pluess, and others) shows that sensory processing sensitivity (SPS) runs in families.
00016	Genes involved in neurotransmitter systems  which regulate reward, mood, and bonding are believed to contribute. These genes affect how reactive a person’s nervous system and brain are to stimuli. There is measurable evidence in how an HSP’s brain processes information and stimuli. Brain imaging studies (like those using fMRI) have found that highly sensitive people show:
00017	More activation in brain regions related to awareness, empathy, and decision-making
00018	Stronger responses to emotional or social cues — like facial expressions or art
00019	This means their brains literally notice and process more information from each experience.
00020	Benefits and Challenges
00021	While highly sensitive people may find it challenging to move through daily life in many respects, it is important to recognize the strengths and benefits they bring to those around them. They include:
00022	Deep empathy and emotional intelligence
00023	Great appreciation for the natural world
00024	Creativity
00025	Strong values and integrity
00026	Many of the benefits associated with being a highly sensitive person tend to be experienced by those around them, while the challenges are often carried primarily by the HSP themselves. Here are some of them:
00027	Low self esteem and feeling misunderstood
00028	Emotional exhaustion
00029	Self criticism
00030	Difficulty handling change or conflict
00031	What Highly Sensitive People Need Most
00032	HSPs thrive in calm predictable environments. Their nervous systems are finely tuned — they take in more sensory and emotional data than most.
00033	However, we know that life can be anything but predictable, so when something unexpected happens, it is important they have the downtime to process and recharge. Even after positive events like parties or celebrations, they may need extra time to regulate in a calm space.
00034	HSPs value deep meaningful relationships. They need partners and friends who understand and accept how they interact with and process the world around them. They look for authenticity and alignment and have a strong sense of values and integrity. Relationships that are consistent with their values are fulfilling while shallow ones are exhausting.
00035	How HSP Impacts Your Relationship
00036	According to Nicole Schiener, MEd, RP (and HSP-Knowledgeable Therapist), the highly sensitive person may find themselves experiencing the following in their relationship/marriage:
00037	Getting easily overwhelmed by arguments, raised voices or expressions of anger or disappointment
00038	Over-giving or over-functioning leading to resentment and burnout
00039	Having stronger emotional reactions than their partner
00040	Personalizing their partner’s emotions
00041	Difficulty with decision making
00042	Craving a deeper connection
00043	Building Connection Through Sensitivity
00044	Schiener says that it’s particularly important for highly sensitive people and their partners to practice the Gottman concept of ‘Turning Towards’ in their relationship. This means responding positively to your partner’s
00045	bids for connection-
00046	any attempts one partner makes to get attention, affection, or emotional connection. This looks like making eye contact, putting away distractions and asking questions to show interest.
00047	The goal, Schiener says, is not to get rid of sensitivity but to deepen understanding and support of it by emphasizing strengths and talking about each other’s needs. Practice small things often to maintain a positive connection and protect against burnout. When misunderstandings occur, make and accept repairs.
00048	The Power of Turning Towards Sensitivity
00049	Highly sensitive individuals bring exceptional depth, empathy, and awareness to their relationships — qualities that, when nurtured, can foster deep emotional intimacy. Integrating Gottman’s concept of ‘turning towards’
00050	highlights how small moments of responsiveness and presence are key to maintaining connection and trust. For HSPs, these micro-interactions serve as emotional anchors, reinforcing security and mutual understanding. By intentionally recognizing and responding to each other’s bids for connection, partners can transform sensitivity into a source of relational strength, resilience, and enduring love.
