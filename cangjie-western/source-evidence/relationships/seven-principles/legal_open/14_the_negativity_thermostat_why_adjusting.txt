00003	The Negativity Thermostat: Why Adjusting the Temperature Early Can Save Your Relationship Later
00004	The secret to keeping things comfortable, calm, and connected is to address the uncomfortable issues that pull you two apart.
00005	Est. reading time: 5 min.
00006	Longitudinal research on newlywed couples discovered that stable and happy couples are more responsive to slight changes in negativity than ailing marriages.
00007	It’s like every relationship has a connection thermostat that signals how well the relationship is doing. Ideally, you want to find that balance between hot and cold so you can feel
00008	secure, connected, and cherished
00009	.
00010	The problem is, like a house, the thermostat is bound to change from time-to-time based on what happens on the outside. Every relationship is going to feel hot or cold.
00011	The more sensitive you are to the emotional temperature changes and work together to return to the comfort zone of connection, the better chance your relationship has at staying positive and healthy.
00012	The relationship thermostat
00013	In my relationship, my thermostat has a burning point of
00014	volcanic anger
00015	and a freezing point of ice-cold distance and indifference. When I am too hot, I am
00016	critical
00017	,
00018	defensive
00019	, and
00020	contemptuous
00021	. I become
00022	emotionally flooded
00023	and say things I don’t believe about my partner and our relationship. It’s like my body is on fire and if my partner gets closer, I’ll burn her.
00024	When I am too cold, I preoccupy myself with work and offer less spontaneous acts of affection throughout the day. I ask
00025	less questions
00026	and keep to myself more. And when my partner expresses something, I am less engaged. At my freezing point, I appear apathetic when she is hurting. That is not the partner she needs in those painful moments.
00027	My spouse has her own hot and cold points, but with different behaviors.
00028	Luckily for us, we rarely get to these extremes because those temperature points are difficult and painful.
00029	One of the hard lessons we had to learn to keep our emotional connection temperature at a more loving level was the importance of addressing things earlier.
00030	The secret of stable relationships: Address things early
00031	This knowledge came from Dr. Gottman’s research, “In marriages that wind up happy and stable, newlywed [spouses] notice lower levels of negativity…In other marriages, [spouses] adapt to and try to accept this negativity, setting their threshold for response at a much higher (more negative) level.”
00032	In relationships that struggle, there is a tendency to tolerate a hotter or colder connection temperature. Dr. Gottman shares, “It’s as if they are saying to themselves, ‘Just ignore this negativity. Don’t respond to it unless it gets much worse.’ Our research shows that this kind of adaptation to negativity is dysfunctional.”
00033	Dr. Gottman goes on to say that, when partners adapt to hotter or colder connection temperatures, it also increases their “threshold” for determining when things are problematic. This means that those partners will feel like
00034	help is not needed
00035	since they’ve increased the acceptable range of negativity.
00036	In my relationship, I like to imagine our thermostat is like our home’s thermostat. If we get emotionally hotter than 73 or colder than 68, that is a cue that we need to readjust our thermostat and reconnect.
00037	Essentially Dr. Gottman’s research indicates that healthy couples are
00038	more nuanced
00039	to changes in the emotional connection. Most importantly, they see the change in relational temperature as a cue to check-in with their partner or open up. They
00040	turn towards
00041	one another.

00055	Practical tools to reset your relationships thermostat
00056	1: Repair
00057	When the temperature in your relationship is uncomfortable, that is a cue that a repair is needed to reset at a more connecting level. To learn more read:
00058	Repair is the Secret Weapon of Emotionally Connected Couples.
00059	2: Discuss Cues of Your Relationship’s Thermostat
00060	It’s helpful to start with discussing what the relationship feels like when things are going well. Talk about how it feels in your body, the thoughts you have, as well as how you engage one another.
00061	In my marriage this includes more humor, physical affection, and a felt sense of being understood when discussing an issue. Outside of conflict, there are lots of positive interactions, and during conflict, we are probably close to the
00062	magic 5:1 ratio
00063	since we tend to use speaker-listener roles.
00064	Then talk about how you think you two get to this place and what helps keep this temperature.
00065	Discuss Disconnection Cues: Use Drs. John and Julie Gottman’s “Relationship Poop Detector” to determine cues that your relationship’s temperature is too hot or too cold. This includes the Four Horsemen, feeling distant and isolated, or not being affectionate and intimate.
00066	The more awareness you have of what pulls you apart, the easier it will be to say, “oh no, we are too hot. Can we talk and reset our temperature please?”
00067	3: The Conflict Toolkit
00068	Sit-down together and dialogue about what’s changed the thermostat using conflict blueprints: To prevent negativity from making things too hot or cold, use the Gottman Conflict Blueprints such as the Gottman-Rapoport and The
00069	Aftermath of a Fight
00070	.
00071	As a speaker, remember to
00072	help your partner understand your side
00073	and give them a
00074	recipe to be successful with you
00075	.
00076	As the listener,
00077	remember to soothe yourself so you can hear your partne
00078	r and
00079	seek to understand
00080	before problem-solving.
00081	4: Proactively Check Your Relationship’s Temperature:
00082	Instead of waiting until your relationship is sweating with negativity or shivering from a cold loneliness, proactively check each other’s emotional temperature daily, “How are we doing today from your perspective? What’s going well? What is something we can do better?” On a weekly or monthly basis, you can use the
00083	State of the Union Meeting
00084	as well as check-in on how you’re well you’re maintaining the
00085	Magic 6-Hours a Week
00086	Finally, do not wait more than 3 days to address an issue that is making the relationship too hot or too cold. The secret to keeping things comfortable is to address the uncomfortable issues that pull you two apart.
