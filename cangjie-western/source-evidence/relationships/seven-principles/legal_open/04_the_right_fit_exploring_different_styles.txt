00003	The Right Fit: Exploring Different Styles of Couples Counseling
00004	The counseling format you choose will depend on your circumstances and relationship needs.
00005	Est. reading time: 3 min.
00006	In the past few years, many couples therapists have begun to offer multiple modalities ranging from
00007	weekly 1-2 hours of couples therapy, “intensive” or “marathon” couples therapy, and weekend couples retreats. We’ll explore the benefits of each type and the differences between them.
00008	Weekly Couples Therapy
00009	This modality is best suited for individual therapy and for couples without complex conditions. Research on frequency and outcome in
00010	couples therapy
00011	suggests that Massed Sessions with Fading may be ideal for many couples. Massed sessions means lots of upfront therapy because couples often arrive in high distress and display a high motivation for change. Once the couple has stabilized, sessions are spread further apart each time to ensure partners practice the skills from therapy in their everyday lives. This model addresses issues up-front and gradually tapers off.
00012	Intensives or Marathon Couples Therapy
00013	Marathon couples therapy refers to therapists counseling couples for extended hours at a time.
00014	Intensives are highly customizable, usually occurring over 10-15 hours with 1-2 therapists, typically
00015	starting on a Friday afternoon and ending on Sunday.
00016	Helping professionals have used intensive therapy for treating critical conditions such as addictions,
00017	family systems in crisis, and post-disaster trauma work. This intensive approach can yield significant results.
00018	Dr. John Gottman
00019	says that to build hope or confidence in couples therapy, the couple has to see rapid, dramatic change in a relatively short time. When done well, an intensive session can demonstrate resolution for even the most gridlocked issues.
00020	As a couples therapist, intensive is my favorite way of working with couples: it matches what research
00021	has successfully shown, it produces results in a short period of time and it gives me and the couple
00022	time to explore and understand deep-seated problems for meaningful progress.
00023	Weekend Couples Retreat
00024	Weekend couples retreats
00025	combine many benefits of weekly and intensive approaches. A retreat can be helpful for premarital couples (possible gift registry idea!), for couples who want to enhance their relationship, and for those who need immediate help but cannot access a private intensive. Couples retreats offer an
00026	experiential, psychoeducational weekend
00027	with a combination of both individual and group attention.
00028	Drs. John and Julie Gottman famously developed a weekend couples retreat called the
00029	Art and Science of Love
00030	(ASL). This two-day program is designed for couples who want an opportunity to get away from
00031	ordinary life to focus on their relationship. Based on over 40 years of research, the curriculum is taught by approved Gottman workshop leaders around the world. The retreat teaches couples key Gottman findings and includes exercises to apply these skills. Research shows that couples who attended the Art and Science of Love experienced significant improvements in their friendship, conflict management, and shared meaning even at a one-year follow up.
00032	Similar to intensives or marathon couples therapy, Art and Science of Love retreats are a chance to give your relationship attention with the guidance of a
00033	Certified Gottman Therapist
00034	. Couples learn about each other while applying research-based tools in a relaxing, romantic setting.
00035	Keep an eye out for
00036	upcoming Art and Science of Love couples retreats
00037	. You will find them throughout the US as well as internationally and they are often held in beautiful retreat spaces. Remember, practice makes perfect, so take the opportunity to refresh your relationship every year with a new ASL retreat in a different part of the world!
