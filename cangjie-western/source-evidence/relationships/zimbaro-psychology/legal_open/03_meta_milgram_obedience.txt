L00005	## Overall finding
L00006	Milgram's famous experiment contained 23 small-sample conditions. We assembled data from the 21 conditions (N = 740) in which obedience involved progression to maximum voltage (overall rate 43.6%) and coded these conditions on 14 properties.
L00007	
L00008	Logistic regression analysis indicated that eight factors influenced the likelihood that teachers continued to the 450 volt shock: the experimenter's directiveness, legitimacy, and consistency; group pressure on the teacher to disobey; the indirectness, proximity, and intimacy of the relation between teacher and learner; and the distance between the teacher and the experimenter.
L00009	
L00010	## Key predictive factors (effect size ordering)
L00011	The most powerful effects, in decreasing order:
L00012	1. Experimenter's non-directiveness (B=-2.79, p<.001): Proceeding to the 450V shock rarely occurs if the authority figure does not give explicit commands to escalate, even if pressure to escalate is coming from fellow teachers.
L00013	2. Group pressure to disobey (B=-2.49, p<.001): Pressure from a group of teachers against obeying substantially decreases obedience (10% vs 46%).
L00014	3. Teacher-Learner intimacy (B=-2.03, p=.003): Participants shocked learners with whom they had an existing social bond at less than one quarter the rate as when the learners were strangers.
L00015	4. Indirectness (B=2.22, p=.001): When the participant only reads word pairs while another administers the shocks, obedience is very high (non-trigger position, 92.5%).
L00016	5. Proximity (linear B=-1.14, p=.001): Closeness between teacher and learner reduces obedience (no feedback 65% -> voice feedback 62.5% -> proximity 40% -> touch 30%).
L00017	6. Distance between experimenter and teacher (B=-1.14, p=.003): Greater distance associated with lesser obedience.
L00018	7. Inconsistency between experimenters (p=.031): A benign experimenter almost completely overrode the power of the standard "bad" experimenter to induce compliance (good/bad experimenter condition: 20%).
L00019	
L00020	## Overrated factors
L00021	- Setting status/prestige (Bridgeport vs Yale): not associated with obedience (p=.614).
L00022	- Authority legitimacy: obedience was actually higher when the authority was "illegitimate" (peer) in the bivariate view, but this was due to confound with non-directiveness.
L00023	- Teacher gender: no independent effect (women 65% bivariate but disappears when controlled).
L00024	- Learner vulnerability (heart trouble): not significant.
L00025	
L00026	## Relationship to Stanford Prison / deindividuation
L00027	The indirectness finding (non-trigger position) is the structural cousin of the Stanford Prison effect: when the harmful act is mediated by another person or by a system/role, personal moral responsibility diffuses, and obedience/escalation becomes much easier. Proximity and intimacy are the antidotes: making the harmed person present, known, and close restores moral agency.
L00028	
L00029	## Practical implication
L00030	Obedience to authority in intimate/relationship systems (e.g., accepting a partner's or an institution's framing of a situation without questioning) is most likely when: (a) authority gives explicit escalating commands, (b) no peer model of refusal is present, (c) the harmed party is distant/stranger/abstract, (d) one's role is mediated ("I just follow orders", "I'm not the one who decided"). Resistance is enabled by: peer refusal models, bringing the harmed party close and personal, refusing the mediated/role cover, and explicit explicit commands being absent.
