L00005	## ELM core
L00006	According to Petty and Cacioppo (1986), a persuasive communication can stimulate recipients to generate issue-relevant thoughts and to change attitudes in line with these thoughts (central route to persuasion). However, when people do not have the ability or the motivation to think about the issues discussed in the message, they may still use cues (e.g., source credibility) that can help them to make a decision without having to think much about the issues at hand (peripheral types of processing).
L00007	
L00008	Central processing occurs when ability and motivation to think are higher. Peripheral processing occurs when ability or motivation is lower.
L00009	
L00010	## Two factors determine route
L00011	1. Ability: Can the person process the argument? (distraction, time pressure, fatigue, complexity)
L00012	2. Motivation: Does the person care enough to process? (personal relevance, need for cognition, accountability)
L00013	
L00014	When ability or motivation is limited, recipients form attitudes based on easy-to-process cues: communicator credibility, attractiveness, number of arguments, consensus ("everyone says so"), mood.
L00015	
L00016	When ability and motivation are high, recipients pay attention to the actual arguments. Strong arguments persuade; weak arguments backfire (reactance).
L00017	
L00018	## Durability difference
L00019	Elaborative processing of arguments increases the probability that attitude change will last over time. Peripheral cue influence is short-lived.
L00020	
L00021	## Sleeper effect implication
L00022	A discounting cue (e.g., "this comes from an unreliable source") suppresses persuasion initially, but over time the cue fades from memory faster than the arguments, so persuasion can INCREASE over time. This means peripheral-source judgments can poison your immediate reaction while the actual content lingers.
L00023	
L00024	## Relationship application
L00025	When you are tired, stressed, hungry, or emotionally flooded (low ability/motivation to elaborate), you process your partner's message peripherally:
L00026	- You judge by tone, timing, who started it, who's "usually right," mood, not by what they actually said.
L00027	- You may dismiss a fair point because it was delivered angrily (source cue) — and then the fair point lingers and changes your view weeks later (sleeper effect).
L00028	- Conversely, when your partner is flooded, they won't hear your carefully reasoned argument; they'll respond to the peripheral cues (your tone, your timing, who's "right" in the room).
L00029	
L00030	To be heard, don't just strengthen arguments — raise the recipient's ability and motivation first: choose a calm time (ability), tie the issue to shared goals (motivation), and separate the message from the messenger so peripheral cues don't contaminate reception.
L00031	
L00032	## Practical diagnostic
L00033	Ask before a hard conversation: "Is my partner currently able and motivated to elaborate?" If either is low, the conversation will be processed peripherally and any agreement reached will be unstable. Defer, or first restore capacity and motivation.
