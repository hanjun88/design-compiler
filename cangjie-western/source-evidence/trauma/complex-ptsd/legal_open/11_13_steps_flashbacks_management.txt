00001	Strategies in times of  flashbacks
00002	1. Say to yourself: "I am having a flashback".
00003	Flashbacks take us into a timeless part of the psyche that 
00004	feels as helpless, hopeless and surrounded by danger as we 
00005	were in childhood. The feelings and sensations you are 
00006	experiencing are past memories that cannot hurt you now.
00007	2. Remind yourself: "I feel afraid, but I am not in danger!" 
00008	“I am safe now, here in the present.“
00009	Remember you are now in the safety of the present, far 
00010	from the danger of the past.
00011	3. Own your right and need to have boundaries. 
00012	Remind yourself that you do not have to allow anyone to 
00013	mistreat you; you are free to leave dangerous situations and 
00014	protest unfair behavior.
00015	4. Speak reassuringly to the Inner Child. 
00016	The child needs to know that you love her unconditionally-
00017	that she can come to you for comfort and protection when 
00018	she feels lost and scared.
00019	5. Deconstruct eternity thinking.
00020	In childhood, fear and abandonment felt endless - a safer 
00021	future was unimaginable. Remember the flashback will pass 
00022	as it has many times before.
00023	6. Remind yourself that you are in an adult body
00024	You have allies, skills and resources to protect you that you 
00025	never had as a child. 
00026	7. Ease back into your body.
00027	•
00028	 Gently ask your body to relax: 
00029	Feel each of your major muscle groups and softly      
00030	encourage them to relax. 
00031	(Tight muscles send unnecessary danger signals to the brain.)
00032	•
00033	 Breathe deeply and slowly.
00034	(Holding the breath also signals danger).
00035	•
00036	 Slow down.
00037	(Rushing presses the psyche's panic button.)
00038	•
00039	 Find a safe place to unwind and soothe yourself.
00040	Wrap yourself in a blanket, hold a stuffed animal,  lie 
00041	down in a bath, take a nap.
00042	•
00043	 Feel the fear in your body without reacting to it.
00044	Fear is just an energy in your body that cannot hurt you if 
00045	you do not run from it or react self-destructively to it.
00046	8. Resist the Inner Critic's Drasticizing and Catastrophizing
00047	• Use 
00048	thought
00049	-
00050	stopping to halt its endless exaggeration of 
00051	danger and constant planning to control the uncontrollable.
00052	•
00053	 Refuse to shame, hate or abandon yourself. 
00054	•
00055	 Channel the anger of self-attack into saying NO to unfair 
00056	self-criticism. 
00057	• Use 
00058	thought
00059	-
00060	substitution: replace negative thinking with a 
00061	memorized list of your qualities and accomplishments.
00062	9. Allow yourself to grieve. 
00063	Flashbacks are opportunities to release old, unexpressed feelings 
00064	of fear, hurt, and abandonment, and to validate - and then 
00065	soothe - the child's past experience of helplessness and 
00066	hopelessness.  Healthy grieving can turn our tears into self-
00067	compassion and our anger into self-protection.
00068	Strategies in times of  flashbacks
00069	10. Cultivate safe relationships and seek support. 
00070	Take time alone when you need it, but don't let shame isolate 
00071	you.  Feeling shame doesn't mean you are shameful. Educate 
00072	your intimates about flashbacks and ask them to help you 
00073	talk and feel your way through them.
00074	11. Learn to identify the triggers that lead to flashbacks. 
00075	Avoid unsafe people, places, activities and triggering mental 
00076	processes. Practice preventive maintenance with these steps 
00077	when triggering situations are unavoidable.
00078	12. Figure out what you are flashing back to. 
00079	Flashbacks are opportunities to discover, validate and heal 
00080	our wounds from past abuse and abandonment. They also 
00081	point to our still unmet developmental needs and can provide 
00082	motivation to get them met.
00083	13. Be patient with a slow recovery process. 
00084	It takes time in the present to become un-adrenalized, and 
00085	considerable time in the future to gradually decrease the 
00086	intensity, duration and frequency of flashbacks. 
00087	Real recovery is a gradually progressive process [often two 
00088	steps forward, one step back], not an attained salvation 
00089	fantasy. Don't beat yourself up for having a flashback.
00090	Pete Walker, from the book Complex PTSD: From Surviving to Thriving
