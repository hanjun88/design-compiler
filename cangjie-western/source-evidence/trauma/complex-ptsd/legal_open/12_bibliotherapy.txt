00004	Bibliotherapy is a term that describes the very real process of being 
00005	positively and therapeutically influenced by what one reads. Bibliotherapy 
00006	can play an enormous role in enhancing recovery from Complex PTSD. I 
00007	usually find that my clients who make the most progress are those who 
00008	augment their therapy sessions with reading homework [recommended by 
00009	me or self-prescribed]. This is especially true of those who further augment 
00010	their reading with journaling about their cognitive and emotional responses 
00011	to it. These processes help build the new physiological and neuronal brain 
00012	circuitry that accompanies the growth of self-compassion and self-
00013	championing.  [For more on Journaltherapy, read the section on verbal 
00014	ventilating in Chapter 5 of my book]. 
00015	Bibliotherapy is especially helpful for individuals like me who grew up in 
00016	dangerous social environments replete with adults who offered little but 
00017	criticism, intimidation and disgust, and devoid of adults who could be 
00018	looked to for safe support and guidance. It was not until later in life, when I 
00019	had actually had quite a few years of group and individual therapy, that I 
00020	realized that my actual recovery process began a full decade before formal 
00021	therapy and was comprised of therapeutic reading and writing I had naturally 
00022	gravitated to. Before the end of this decade of bibliotherapy and 
00023	journaltherapy, I was too much in denial to know that I’d been grievously 
00024	injured in my family, and I was way beyond even considering the idea of 
00025	going into formal therapy and exposing my vulnerabilities to another human 
00026	being. Instead I sought the help of others in the many spiritual and 
00027	psychological self-help books I was somewhat unconsciously drawn to. 
00028	Without really understanding it, I not only gained valuable insights about 
00029	how to improve the way I treated myself and others, but equally importantly, 
00030	I gleaned that there were numerous good, safe, wise and helpful adults out 
00031	there who could be trusted and who had a great deal of wise and kind 
00032	guidance to offer. Over time the authors of these books seemed like a small 
00033	tribe of elders who I began to imagine as people who would have empathy 
00034	for me had I occasion to meet them. Eventually when I achieved something 
00035	of a critical mass of this awareness, I managed to take what seemed at the 
00036	time a blind, frightening leap into the water of therapy, and lucked out – a 
00037	“luck” informed by all I had read – and got a good enough therapist to help 
00038	me take steps in my healing that I could not manage on my own [Please also 
00039	see “Finding a Therapist” on this website.]
