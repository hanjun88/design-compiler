L0001  [Nancy case, 1969] "When Nancy arrived, her heart was beating frantically, at about 120 beats a minute, and she had the frozen deer-in-the-headlights look."
L0002  "I saw a tiger crouching, ready to spring forth ... 'there is a tiger coming after you Nancy, run, run climb those rocks and escape.'"
L0003  "After about 30 minutes of gentle shaking and trembling, she began to breathe more fully and spontaneously the color of her cheeks flushed ... She felt like she was 'being held by a warm, tingling wave.'"
L0004  Trauma happens when the energy that we gather to defend and protect ourselves doesn't get used up or doesn't get to complete its whole cycle. So, if we wanted to run and couldn't run, if we wanted to fight and couldn't fight, that energy gets stuck inside of us.
L0005  "In this emotional and anxious state, the now frustrated fight response erupts into a rage; the frustrated flight response gives way to helplessness" (Levine 1997, p.98).
L0006  If neither fight nor flight is available, the animal goes to freeze ... to trick a predator into believing they are dead. The object is to stay alive until the danger is past and deal with the consequences later.
L0007  At that point, the animal will eventually shake and tremble, discharging all of that fight or flight energy.
L0008  However, if the animal remains stuck in this state for an extended period of time the energy is not discharged. This activation ... can stay on for years until the body is able to complete the responses that it began or wanted to do at that time.
L0009  "Trauma is a highly activated incomplete biological response to a threat, frozen in time" (Payne et al. 2015, p.14). Undischarged energy is what gives rise to trauma symptoms.
L0010  Our guts twist up, our muscles tighten, and heartbeat starts racing or, conversely, slowing ... But, unlike the wild animals, we don't release this shock reaction. Traumatized people get stuck in a vicious cycle.
L0011  There are four components of trauma that will always be present to some degree in any traumatized individual: hyperarousal, constriction, dissociation, and freezing or immobility (sense of helplessness).
L0012  In order to avoid hyperarousal, we want to complete an entire arousal cycle ... If we allow ourselves to have these sensations, individuals will peak and they will finish, resolving the arousal cycle.
L0013  As this process occurs, we may experience "trembling, shaking, vibration, waves of warmth, the fullness of breath, slowed heart rate, relaxation of the muscles, and the overall feeling of relief, comfort and safety" (Levine 1997, p.128).
L0014  Dissociation ... "enables a person to endure experiences that are at the moment beyond endurance" (Levine 1997, p.138).
L0015  "If hyperarousal is the nervous system's accelerator, the sense of immobility and freezing overwhelming helplessness is its brakes" (Levine 1997, p.142).
L0016  There are three methods used in SE to guide the nervous system toward increased equilibrium: Resourcing, Pendulation, and Titration.
L0017  Resourcing: "to put a person in touch with positive inner feelings of safety, strength, comfort, and optimism" (Payne et al. 2015, p.8). Stressed and traumatized individuals are more likely to focus on negative interoceptive cues.
L0018  Titration simply means that we slow things down because trauma is "too much, too fast, and too soon." ... "drop by drop, so as to avoid unnecessary distress, flooding, and potential re-traumatization" (Payne et al. 2015, p.10).
L0019  Pendulation involves the shifting of body sensations, going back and forth between charge/activation and discharge/deactivation ... A resilient nervous system is one that can ebb and flow between alertness and action, and calm and rest without getting stuck at either extreme.
L0020  Many therapeutic trauma approaches will encourage the survivor to repetitively relive their traumas in great detail ... If the therapist keeps the client in a state of high fear and physiological arousal, it is likely that their painful past trauma can actually be reinforced.
L0021  SE is ... a "bottom-up" approach to healing rather than ... talk therapy, a "top-down" approach. The aim is to access the body memory of the event, not the story, and work to heal and release the embodied trauma patterns.
L0022  Wild prey animals ... most animals have a similar physiological process of returning to normal after a narrow escape ... shaking, trembling, and spontaneous breathing ... Levine also observed this in many Shamanic rituals performed throughout different parts of the world.
L0023  The Shaman treats traumatized individuals by helping them coax their spirits back into their bodies ... sometimes called soul retrieval in the presence of the community because the connection of friends and family is an integral part of healing.
L0024  "When good SE therapists are working, even with someone with severe trauma, they're guided by a trust that we all have the innate capacity to heal from even the most horrific events" (Simon 2019, p.4).
